Game day

What to pack in your rec league game-day bag

The essentials, sport-by-sport extras, and a small shared kit that saves the day for the whole team.

By the Toss Sports team · Last updated September 23, 2026 · 4 min read

There is always one player who turns up without water, in the wrong colour shirt, with shoes that slide on the gym floor. Do not be that player. A well-packed game-day bag takes five minutes to put together and saves you from an uncomfortable evening. Here is a practical packing list for adult rec league games, from the essentials everyone needs to the sport-specific extras.

The essentials: every sport, every week

These are the items that live in the bag permanently, or at least get checked before every game.

  • Water bottle, full. Even low-intensity games like cornhole happen outside in summer heat.
  • Team shirt or the colour your captain asked for.
  • Appropriate shoes for the surface (grass, turf, gym floor or lanes).
  • Phone and a charger or battery pack, for the group chat, the venue map and the score.
  • ID and a payment method, especially if the league has a partner bar.
  • A light layer, because evening games get cold once the sun goes down.
  • A small towel.
  • Any personal medication you might need, such as an inhaler.
  • A plastic bag for sweaty or wet clothes on the way home.

Sport-specific extras

Each sport has a few extra items that make a real difference. Most leagues provide the main equipment, so this list focuses on personal gear.

SportUseful extras
CornholeYour own bags only if your league allows personal sets; a hand towel to dry bags on damp evenings; a pen for the scoresheet.
KickballMolded cleats or grippy trainers, sunscreen, a hat, a spare pair of socks for wet grass.
PickleballPaddle, court shoes with lateral support, a sweatband, sunglasses for outdoor courts.
BowlingYour own ball and shoes if you have them; socks if you are using rentals; a small towel for your hands.
DodgeballIndoor court shoes with non-marking soles, knee pads if you like to dive, a spare shirt.
Flag footballMolded cleats, shorts without pockets (pockets can snag flags), a mouthguard if you want one, gloves with grip.
VolleyballKnee pads, court shoes, and sand socks for beach courts.

Weather-proofing outdoor games

Most outdoor rec leagues play in whatever the evening throws at them, short of lightning. Keep a few weather items in the bag during the season:

  • Sun: sunscreen, a cap or visor, sunglasses and extra water.
  • Rain: a light waterproof layer, a spare pair of socks and a bag to keep your phone dry.
  • Cold: a warm layer for the bench, and a hat for after the game.
  • Bugs: insect repellent for dusk games near grass or water.

If there is thunder or lightning, follow your league's policy. Most stop play and move people indoors or to cars until the storm has passed.

The captain's team kit

If you captain a team, or just like being the organised one, a small shared kit can rescue a game. It does not need to be big. A tote bag or box in the car boot works.

Team kit

  • A basic first-aid kit with plasters, tape, antiseptic wipes and a cold pack
  • Spare team shirts or pinnies in your colour
  • A printed copy of the league rules and the team roster with phone numbers
  • A pump and needle, if your sport uses an inflatable ball
  • Spare water and a few snacks
  • Sunscreen and bug spray to share
  • A marker and tape, for makeshift numbers or field lines

For the logistics side of game day (confirming times, assigning jobs, leaving notes for next week) our printable game-day checklist pairs nicely with this list.

Food and drink

For an evening game, a normal meal a couple of hours beforehand works for most people. If you are coming straight from work, a light snack such as a banana or granola bar in the bag keeps you going. Drink water before, during and after, especially in warm weather. If your league heads to a bar afterwards, eat something first, and remember the drive home.

Pack it the night before

The easiest way to never forget anything is to keep the essentials in the bag permanently and restock straight after each game: refill the water bottle, wash the shirt, replace used first-aid items. On game day, all you need to add is your phone and a good attitude.

FAQ

Do I need cleats for kickball or flag football?

They help on grass, but athletic shoes with good grip are fine for most social leagues. If you buy cleats, choose molded rubber or plastic ones, since metal cleats are commonly banned.

Should I bring my own equipment?

Most leagues provide game balls, boards and nets. Personal items like pickleball paddles, bowling balls or knee pads are optional, but many players like having their own.

What should a team first-aid kit include?

Basics such as adhesive bandages, gauze, tape, antiseptic wipes, a cold pack and gloves are a sensible start. Check the venue's own first-aid arrangements too, and know where the nearest help is.

How do I stop my bag from smelling?

Take wet or sweaty clothes out as soon as you get home, let shoes air-dry out of the bag, and wash the bag itself every few weeks.
