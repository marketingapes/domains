What SSDI is

Social Security Disability Insurance pays monthly benefits to people who cannot work because of a medical condition that is expected to last at least a year or result in death — and who have worked long enough, and recently enough, paying into Social Security. It is a federal program; the Social Security Administration decides every claim.

A condition that stops work

The condition must prevent substantial work and be expected to last 12 months or longer, or be terminal. SSA keeps a listing of impairments, but a condition does not have to match a listing exactly to qualify.

Work credits matter

SSDI is insurance you paid into through payroll taxes. How many work credits you need depends on your age; as an example, many adults need roughly 5 years of work in the last 10. The reviewing firm checks the real rules for your age.

Denials can be appealed

Many first applications are denied. A denial is not the end: there is an appeals process, and the first appeal deadline is generally 60 days from the denial notice. If you were denied, choose the appeals path in the form above.

Is YOUR BRAND HERE a law firm?

Is this the same as applying with Social Security?

No. This is a private review service. You can always apply directly with the Social Security Administration for free at ssa.gov, whether or not you use this page.

I was already denied. Is it too late?

Often not — denials can be appealed, generally within 60 days of the denial notice. Choose the appeals path in the form and a reviewer can look at your timing.
