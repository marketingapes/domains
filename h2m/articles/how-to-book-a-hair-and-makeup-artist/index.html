How to Book a Hair and Makeup Artist for an Event (Without the Stress)

By the Hair 2 Makeup team · Last updated 23 September 2026

Booking a hair and makeup artist can feel like dating: lots of scrolling, a few messages, and a nagging worry that you have picked the wrong one. The process is much simpler if you know what to look for and what to ask. Whether it is a wedding, a prom, a milestone birthday or a photo shoot, the steps below help you choose someone whose work you love and whose logistics fit your day.

Start with the basics: date, headcount, location

Before you contact anyone, pin down three facts. Artists need them to tell you whether they are even available, and your first message will get a faster, more useful reply.

  • Date and ready-by time. Not the ceremony time: the time everyone needs to be finished and dressed.
  • How many people want hair, makeup or both. Be honest about maybes; you can confirm later.
  • Where you will get ready, including whether there is parking, a lift and decent natural light.

For weddings on popular dates, good artists are often booked many months ahead, so start early if your date is on a weekend in peak season. For proms and parties, a few weeks to a couple of months is often enough, but popular dates like prom night fill up fast locally because everyone needs the same evening.

Where to look and how to judge a portfolio

Recommendations from friends, your venue or your photographer are a great starting point because they come with real-world context. Social media portfolios are useful too, but look at them critically:

  • Look for work on people with skin tones and hair textures similar to yours. Range matters, and a good portfolio shows it.
  • Look for unfiltered or lightly edited photos, ideally from real events rather than only styled shoots.
  • Check that the artist regularly does the type of look you want, whether that is soft and natural, full glam or a vintage set.
  • Notice whether hair and makeup are done by the same person or a team; both are fine, but it affects timing and price.

Questions to ask before you pay anything

Pay attention to how the artist replies as well as what they say. Clear, prompt answers are a good sign of how organised the day itself will be.

Understanding the contract and deposit

A written agreement protects you and the artist. It does not need to be long, but it should state the date, start time, address, number of people and services, the total price and what it includes, the deposit and when the balance is due, and the cancellation terms on both sides. Look for what happens if the artist is ill: some have a backup artist they work with, which is reassuring.

Deposits are normal and usually non-refundable because the artist turns away other work for your date. Pay by a traceable method and keep the receipt. If anything in the contract is unclear, ask before signing rather than after.

Booking a group: keeping everyone in the loop

If you are booking for a bridal party or a group of friends, decide early who pays for what. Some couples cover everyone; in other groups each person pays their own share. Either way, one person should act as the main contact with the artist so messages do not get lost. Collect everyone's service choices (hair, makeup or both) and any allergies in one place, then send a single confirmed list.

Red flags worth noticing

  • No written agreement, or reluctance to put prices in writing.
  • A portfolio that seems to show only one skin tone or hair type when yours is different.
  • Pressure to pay the full amount immediately to "hold" a date.
  • Vague answers about hygiene, such as whether brushes are cleaned between clients.

None of these is automatically disqualifying, but each is worth a follow-up question. Good artists are usually happy to explain how they work.

Frequently asked questions

How far ahead should I book for a wedding?

As soon as you have a date and venue, especially for weekends in popular months. Earlier booking gives you the most choice.

Should I tip my hair and makeup artist?

Customs vary by country and by whether the person owns the business. If you are unsure, check what is typical locally or ask the artist whether gratuity is included.

Can one artist do hair and makeup for a big group?

Sometimes, but it takes time. For larger groups many artists bring an assistant or a second artist. Ask how they would handle your headcount.

What if I need to add a person later?

Tell your artist as early as possible. Adding people is often possible but depends on their schedule on the day.
