Skin Prep Before an Event: A Calm Countdown From Weeks Out to the Morning Of

By the Hair 2 Makeup team · Last updated 23 September 2026

Great makeup starts with comfortable, hydrated skin. The good news is that event skin prep is mostly about consistency and avoiding surprises, not about expensive last-minute treatments. This countdown focuses on simple, widely recommended habits and the common mistakes that cause redness or breakouts right before a big day.

Please note: this is general information, not medical or dermatological advice. If you have a skin condition, are using prescription skincare, or are unsure whether a treatment is right for you, speak to a dermatologist or qualified professional.

The golden rule: nothing brand new close to the day

The most common pre-event skin drama comes from trying something new: a fresh exfoliating acid, an unfamiliar face mask or a strong treatment a few days before. Even products that suit most people can cause redness, dryness or irritation on some skin, and there may not be time for it to settle. As a general rule, introduce any new product well in advance, and patch-test it first as the packaging directs. In the final couple of weeks, stick with what you know your skin tolerates.

Four to eight weeks out

  • Settle on a simple routine: a gentle cleanser, a moisturiser that suits your skin, and daily sunscreen. Consistency tends to do more than complexity.
  • Book professional treatments early. If you want a facial or any in-clinic treatment, ask the provider about recovery time and schedule it with plenty of room before the event. Many people choose to avoid anything new in the final fortnight.
  • If you use prescription or strong actives, ask your prescriber how to handle them around the event rather than stopping or doubling up on your own.
  • Plan hair removal. Waxing, threading or other methods can leave skin red for a while. Test your method well before and schedule the final session a few days out, not the morning of.

The final week

If a breakout appears, leave it alone as much as possible and let your artist know. Concealing a flat, calm spot is much easier than covering one that has been squeezed.

The night before

This is not the evening for a dramatic mask or a new eye cream. Cleanse gently, moisturise and go to bed at a reasonable time. Some people find that sleeping with their head slightly raised helps with morning puffiness. Lay out your morning skincare so you are not searching for it in a busy room. Alcohol and very salty food can leave some people puffy the next day, so you may want to go easy on them.

The morning of: prepping skin for makeup

Unless your artist gives other instructions, a simple sequence works well:

  1. Cleanse with lukewarm water and your usual gentle cleanser.
  2. Hydrate with a lightweight moisturiser and give it several minutes to absorb before any makeup goes on. Rushing this step is a common cause of foundation that slides or pills.
  3. Lip balm on early so lips are smooth by the time colour goes on; blot it off before lipstick.
  4. Eye area: a cold spoon or chilled cloth can feel soothing on puffy eyes. Keep heavy eye creams light so concealer does not crease.
  5. Sunscreen: if you will be outside, use it. Mention it to your artist, as some formulas can affect how flash photos look, and they may have a preferred type.

Matching prep to your skin type

Don't forget the rest of you

Hands, neck and chest often appear in photos too. Moisturise them, and if you are wearing an open neckline, ask whether your artist will blend base or bronzer there to keep your face and chest looking like the same person. Tidy nails and a hand cream that has had time to absorb help ring photos look their best.

Frequently asked questions

Should I get a facial the week before?

Many people prefer to avoid anything new in the final one to two weeks. If you want a professional treatment, book it earlier and ask the provider about recovery time.

What if I break out the day before?

Try not to touch it, keep your routine gentle and tell your makeup artist. Calm, flat skin is much easier to conceal.

Can I self-tan before the event?

Yes, but test the product beforehand and apply it a few days ahead so it can settle. Tell your artist so they can match your base.

Is this medical advice?

No. It is general information. For skin conditions or prescription products, consult a qualified professional.
