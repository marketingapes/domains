Updo, Half-Up or Down? How to Choose Your Event Hairstyle

By the Hair 2 Makeup team · Last updated 23 September 2026

It is often the first question a stylist asks and the one people agonise over most: up or down? There is no universally flattering answer, but there is a right answer for your outfit, your hair and your day. Work through the factors below and the choice usually becomes clear.

Start with your neckline and back

Your outfit is the biggest clue, because hair either frames or covers the neckline and back.

These are starting points, not rules. If you love your hair down and your outfit has a high neck, a half-up style or a sleek low ponytail over one shoulder can give you both.

Think about your hair itself

  • Length: very short hair is often best styled down with texture or accessories; shoulder-length can do low buns and half-up styles; long hair can do almost anything but gets heavy in intricate updos.
  • Density: thick hair holds updos well but can feel heavy; fine hair may need texturising product or padding for volume in an updo, and loose curls may drop faster.
  • Texture: natural curls and coils can be the star of the show in down or half-up styles, or set in beautiful textured updos. A stylist experienced with your texture is key.
  • Extensions: clip-ins can add length or fullness to either style. Tell your stylist in advance so they can plan the placement.

Weather and venue

Where you will be matters as much as what you wear. Hair that is down is more affected by humidity, wind and heat; curls can drop and frizz can appear. If your event is outdoors in summer, on a beach or in a humid climate, an updo or half-up style is often more reliable. In cooler weather or an air-conditioned venue, down styles hold much better. Rain is a wildcard either way, so have an umbrella and a plan.

Comfort across a long day

Updos can feel tight or heavy after many hours, especially with a veil or heavy accessories. Down styles can feel hot and get in the way while eating or dancing. Ask yourself:

  • Do I often get headaches from tight ponytails or lots of pins?
  • Will I get hot? Is the venue warm or will I be dancing a lot?
  • Will I be comfortable with hair on my neck for ten hours?
  • Am I happy to have hair touching my face in photos when it is windy?

Your trial is the best place to test comfort. Wear the style for as long as you can afterwards and notice how you feel.

The half-up compromise

Half-up styles take the top section back and leave the rest loose. They keep hair off the face, show off lengths and curls, and are usually more comfortable than a full updo. They also work well with veils, flowers and hair vines. The main trade-off is the same as with hair down: the loose part is affected by humidity and wind.

Photos and your personal style

Think about how you want to look back on the pictures. Classic updos tend to feel timeless, while very on-trend styles can date more quickly. Most importantly, choose something that feels like you. If you always wear your hair down, a dramatic updo might make you feel unlike yourself; a soft, loose updo or a half-up style might be the sweet spot.

If you still can't decide, ask your stylist whether you can try two options at your trial, or whether a style can transition from up to down later in the evening. A down style can also be turned into a loose low bun with a few pins when the dancing starts.

Frequently asked questions

Is an updo better for photos?

Not automatically. Updos show off necklines and stay neat in wind, but hair down can look beautifully soft. Choose what suits your outfit and you.

Will my curls last if I wear my hair down?

They last longer when set properly and in cooler, drier conditions. In humidity, a half-up style or updo is often more reliable.

Can I switch from up to down during the event?

Sometimes. Ask your stylist during the trial whether the style can be let down neatly, as heavy product and pins can make this tricky.

Does a veil work with hair down?

Yes. Veils can be anchored to a small section pinned at the crown, or to a half-up style.
