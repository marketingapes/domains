12 Mini Golf Party Games That Make Every Round Unpredictable

By the Crazy Golf Game team · Last updated 23 September 2026 · 3 min read

Standard stroke play is fine, but mini golf truly shines when you bend the rules. Party variations level the field between strong and weak players, keep everyone laughing, and give a group something to talk about afterwards. These twelve games work on commercial courses (as long as you keep pace with other groups) and on backyard or living-room layouts.

Games that change how you putt

  1. Wrong-hand hole. On nominated holes, everyone putts left-handed if they are right-handed, and vice versa. It is humbling for good players and gives beginners a real chance.
  2. One-handed round. Only one hand on the putter for the whole hole. Choose which hand before teeing off.
  3. Croquet style. Stand side-on and putt the ball between your feet, facing the hole, like a croquet swing. Only on safe, uncrowded holes.
  4. Eyes closed. Line up your putt, then close your eyes before the stroke. A friend tells you when the ball has stopped. Great on straight, short holes.

Team formats

  1. Scramble. Teams of two to four. Everyone putts from the tee, the team picks the best ball, and all players putt again from that spot. Repeat until someone holes out. This is the friendliest format for mixed abilities because nobody's bad shot counts.
  2. Alternate shot. Pairs share one ball and take turns hitting it. It rewards communication and produces plenty of comedy when one partner leaves the other in a terrible spot.
  3. Best ball. Everyone plays their own ball, and the team counts only the lowest score on each hole. It is faster than a scramble and still forgiving.
  4. Relay. Split into two teams. Each player takes one stroke, then hands the putter to the next teammate. The first team to finish all holes wins.

Games that add chance

  1. Challenge cards. Write silly rules on index cards: "putt from your knees", "sing while you putt", "your opponent chooses your ball's starting spot". Each player draws one per hole. The random hole generator on our home page can supply ideas.
  2. Dice par. Roll a die at each tee. The number becomes par for that hole. Anyone who beats the rolled par earns a point.
  3. Swap balls. After everyone tees off, each player plays the next shot from the ball of the person on their left.
  4. Bounty hole. Nominate one hole where a hole-in-one earns a prize, such as choosing the next game or skipping the washing-up.

Scoring party games without arguments

Party games need slightly different scoring from standard play. For formats where the numbers get silly, switch to match play: each hole is a mini contest, and the lowest score wins one point. Ties split the point or carry it over to the next hole. The team or player with the most points at the end wins. This stops one disastrous hole from wrecking someone's whole afternoon.

A second option is a points ladder: three points for an ace, two for par or better, one for finishing within the stroke limit, and zero otherwise. Kids tend to love this because they can see their points rising even if they are not winning outright.

Keeping it fun on a public course

If you are playing at a commercial course, keep the silliness compatible with other players. Choose games that do not slow you down; team scramble and alternate shot can be quick, but eyes-closed putting on every hole will back up the queue. Stay on the playing surface, keep noise reasonable, and let faster groups through. Never use challenges that involve climbing on features or swinging hard.

At home you can go wild. Set up a trophy made from a spray-painted plastic cup, give out a "most creative miss" award, and let the winner design a hole for next time. For a full event format with brackets and handicaps, see how to run a mini golf tournament at home.

Party planning checklist

  • Pick two or three games in advance and explain them at the first tee.
  • Write challenge cards before guests arrive.
  • Bring a pencil and a spare scorecard for match-play points.
  • Decide the tie-breaker before you start.
  • Have a small, silly prize ready for the winner and a second one for the funniest moment.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best mini golf game for a large group?

Split into teams of three or four and play a scramble or best ball. Everyone stays involved, and teams finish holes quickly.

How do you make mini golf fair for kids and adults together?

Let kids tee off closer, use a scramble format, or give adults a handicap such as wrong-hand putting on certain holes.

Can we play these games at a commercial course?

Most can, as long as they do not slow the course down or involve climbing or unsafe swings. Check the course rules first.

How many challenge cards should I make?

Around twenty cards covers an 18-hole round for a small group without too many repeats.

Play safe: swing low near people, pets and windows, and follow any posted course rules.
