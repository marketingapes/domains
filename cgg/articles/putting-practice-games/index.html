10 Putting Practice Games That Feel Like Play, Not Drills

By the Crazy Golf Game team · Last updated 23 September 2026 · 4 min read

Most people do not practise putting because practising putting sounds boring. Hitting the same three-foot putt fifty times is dull. But turn it into a game with a score, a streak or a friend to beat, and it becomes something you look forward to. These ten games work on a carpet at home, a putting mat, a practice green at a golf course, or a backyard lawn. Each one sharpens a real skill: speed, line or pressure.

Games for aim and start line

1. The gate. Push two tees into the ground (or place two coins on carpet) just wider than your putter head, about 15 cm in front of the ball. Putt through the gate without touching it. Score one point per clean pass, and move the gate to a narrower spacing after five in a row. It trains a square putter face at impact.

2. Around the clock. Place six or eight balls in a circle about 90 cm (three feet) around the cup, like numbers on a clock. Hole them in order. Miss one and start again. On a slope, each position plays differently, which teaches you to read breaks from every side.

3. Coin target. On a flat carpet, put a coin down and try to stop the ball on top of it from two metres. It is harder than holing out because the ball has to stop exactly there, which is a great exercise for aim and pace together.

Games for speed and distance

4. Ladder. Place tees or coins at 1, 2, 3 and 4 metres from you. Putt one ball to stop just past each marker, in order. Then come back down. If a ball rolls past the next marker up, start again. It is the best single exercise for touch.

5. Lag bowling. Put a towel or a line of tape across the far end of a hallway or practice green. Take turns rolling balls as close to the line as possible without crossing it, like bowls. Closest ball scores a point. Brilliant with two or more players.

6. Fringe stop. On a real green, putt from one side toward the far fringe and try to stop the ball within a putter length of the edge. Missing short or rolling off both lose a point.

Games for pressure

7. Putting H-O-R-S-E. Like the basketball game. One player picks a putt, from any spot, with any rule ("left hand only"). If they make it, the next player must match it or earn a letter. Five letters and you are out.

8. Streak. Pick a distance you make most of the time. Count how many in a row you can hole. Try to beat your record every session. The last few putts of a long streak are surprisingly nerve-racking, which is exactly the point.

9. Up and down. Drop a ball at random within a few metres of the cup. You must hole out in two putts or fewer. Nine balls make a "round"; count how many you complete.

10. Twenty-one. Two or more players take turns from a set spot. Holing out scores three points, a ball finishing within a putter-head of the cup scores one. First to twenty-one wins, but going over drops you back to fifteen.

Building a 15-minute session

A short session done often beats a long one done rarely. A simple structure: five minutes of the gate to groove your stroke, five minutes of ladder for speed, and five minutes of a pressure game to finish. Pick one skill to focus on per session rather than trying to fix everything. If you like planning ahead, our practice session planner walks you through choosing a focus and noting what worked.

Practising at home without a green

A long hallway rug or a roll-out putting mat is plenty. Carpet tends to be slower than a real green, so do not worry if distances feel different; the rhythm and aim still transfer. Use a mug on its side or a tape circle as a cup. For households with children, lag bowling and H-O-R-S-E turn practice into family game night, and kids are often better at them than adults.

Once your touch improves, take it to the mini golf course and try the techniques in reading banks and slopes.

Frequently asked questions

How often should I practise putting?

Short, frequent sessions work well for most casual golfers. Even ten to fifteen minutes a few times a week can make a noticeable difference to feel and confidence.

Can I practise putting on carpet?

Yes. Carpet is usually slower than a green, but it is great for grooving a consistent stroke and aim.

What is the single best putting game?

For most casual players, the ladder, because distance control saves the most strokes on both mini golf and regular courses.

Do these games work for kids?

Absolutely. Lag bowling, H-O-R-S-E and twenty-one are especially good with children because they are competitive and quick.

Play safe: swing low near people, pets and windows, and follow any posted course rules.
