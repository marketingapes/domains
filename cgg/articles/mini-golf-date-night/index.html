Mini Golf Date Night: Ideas, Friendly Wagers and Easy Conversation

By the Crazy Golf Game team · Last updated 23 September 2026 · 4 min read

Mini golf is one of the most reliable date ideas around, and for good reasons. You stand side by side instead of across a table, there is always something to talk about, and nobody expects either of you to be good at it. Missing a putt spectacularly is funny, not embarrassing. Whether it is a first date or your five-hundredth, here is how to make a mini golf night a good one.

Why mini golf works so well as a date

Conversation on a first date can stall when there is nothing to do but stare at a menu. Mini golf gives you natural pauses, shared moments and lots of small things to react to: the windmill, the awkward bounce, the unexpected hole-in-one. It is also active without being sweaty, and it has a clear beginning and end, so it takes the pressure off planning the whole evening.

For couples who have been together a while, it is a break from the dinner-and-a-film routine. A little friendly competition can be surprisingly revealing: you will quickly learn who is a gracious winner and who needs a rematch immediately.

Planning the evening

  • Pick the course: outdoor courses at dusk are atmospheric; indoor glow-in-the-dark courses work year round and are often open late.
  • Check the basics: opening hours, whether you can book ahead or just turn up, and whether the course gets busy with families earlier in the evening.
  • Plan the "after": a nearby café, dessert spot or walk means the date can continue if it is going well, or end naturally if not.
  • Dress for the weather: comfortable shoes and a layer for outdoor courses once the sun goes down.
  • Keep it light: this is not the night to show off your professional putting stroke.

Keep the stakes to things that are fun, small and entirely optional. If your date does not seem keen on betting, drop it; the game is enjoyable enough on its own.

Games that spark conversation

A standard round works, but a couple of twists can make it more memorable:

  1. Question holes. Before each hole, the player with the honour (who won the last hole) asks a question. Keep them light early, such as "best holiday you have ever had", and more reflective later.
  2. Handicap trading. If one of you is clearly better, the stronger player putts one-handed or wrong-handed on alternate holes.
  3. Design a hole. On the walk between holes, describe the obstacle you would build if you owned the course. It is a surprisingly good window into someone's imagination.
  4. Alternate shot. Share one ball and take turns. It is cooperative, and bad shots become shared jokes rather than personal failures.

Mini golf etiquette on a date

Be generous. Offer to let your date go first, compliment good shots genuinely, and laugh at your own bad ones. If they are new to the game, share a tip only if they seem to want one; nobody enjoys being coached on a date. Keep pace with other groups, especially families with kids, and keep phones in pockets except for a quick photo at the signature hole.

The at-home version

Staying in? Build a three- or four-hole course around the living room with cushions, books and mugs, dim the lights, and add string lights or a torch-lit final hole. Make a handwritten scorecard, pour a drink, and play for who cooks next time. It is cheap, creative and a lot more memorable than scrolling through streaming menus. Feeling brave? Close the evening with a "trick shot showdown": each person gets three attempts at a silly shot of their own design, and the other judges. Our beginner trick shots article has safe ideas to start with.

Frequently asked questions

Is mini golf a good first date?

For many people, yes. It is relaxed, affordable at most courses, gives you things to talk about and has a natural end point. It suits people who enjoy light activity more than long sit-down conversations.

What if my date is much better at mini golf than me?

Suggest a team format like alternate shot or give the stronger player a handicap. Or simply enjoy being bad at it; that is half the charm.

What should I wear for a mini golf date?

Comfortable shoes and weather-appropriate clothing. Outdoor courses can get cool after sunset, so bring a layer.

How long does a mini golf date last?

An 18-hole round often takes somewhere around an hour, depending on the course and how busy it is, which leaves time for food or a walk afterwards.

Play safe: swing low near people, pets and windows, and follow any posted course rules.
