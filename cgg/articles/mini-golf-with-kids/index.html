Mini Golf With Kids: Age-by-Age Tips for a Happy Round

By the Crazy Golf Game team · Last updated 9 October 2026 · 4 min read

Mini golf is one of the few outings that toddlers, teenagers and grandparents can all enjoy together. It is short, it is outdoors or in a bright indoor hall, and there is something to look at on every hole. It can also go sideways fast: a tired four-year-old, an argument about who went first, a ball in the pond. A little planning makes a big difference. Here is how to keep the round fun at every age.

Before you go: set the tone

Kids take their cue from adults. If the grown-ups are keen to win, the children will feel it. Decide in advance that the goal is fun and that scores are optional for younger players. Tell kids about the course before you arrive: "there is a pirate ship hole and a waterfall". Anticipation is half the fun.

Choose your time wisely. Weekday late afternoons or early weekend mornings tend to be quieter at many courses, which means less waiting and more patience from the groups behind you. Check whether the course has shade, toilets and somewhere to sit, and bring water and a snack.

Age-by-age guide

Teaching the basics in one minute

Children do not need a lecture. Show them three things at the first tee and then let them play:

  1. Hands together on the grip, like holding a lollipop with both hands.
  2. Stand beside the ball, not behind it, with feet apart.
  3. Swing like a clock pendulum, tick-tock, putter low to the ground.

Kids often lift the putter high and hit down on the ball, making it hop. A simple cue is "keep the putter touching the grass-carpet on the way back." If they are struggling, putt alongside them with your own ball so they can copy your rhythm.

Make sure their putter fits. Most courses provide shorter children's putters. A putter that is too long forces kids to stand far away and swing awkwardly; one that roughly reaches their hip when the head is flat on the ground is about right.

Scoring that keeps everyone happy

For under-sevens, consider not keeping score at all, or keep it privately. For older kids, use a stroke limit so no one suffers a nine on a hole. Many families give children a lower limit, such as five strokes, than the adults. Another option is points: an ace earns three, par earns two, finishing earns one. Every child finishes each hole with something to celebrate.

If you have competitive siblings, team formats are a lifesaver. Pair each child with an adult or put all the kids against all the grown-ups in a scramble. It turns rivalry into cooperation.

Safety habits worth teaching

  • Only the player whose turn it is holds the putter up; everyone else keeps putter heads on the ground.
  • Stand behind the person putting, never in front or right beside them.
  • Walk, do not run, between holes, especially near water features and steps.
  • Wait for the group ahead to leave the hole before putting.
  • Do not climb on obstacles or reach into water features to get a ball; ask an adult or staff member.

Keeping attention for the whole course

Eighteen holes can be a lot for small children. It is perfectly fine to play nine and head for an ice cream. If you want to finish, build in little missions: spot every animal on the course, guess which hole will be hardest, give the scorecard to a different child every three holes. Letting a restless child "caddy" for an adult, carrying the ball to the next tee, also helps.

Bad holes happen. If a child is getting frustrated, quietly let them place the ball close to the cup, or offer a "magic putt" do-over. The aim is for them to want to come back.

Bringing the fun home

Frequently asked questions

What age can children start playing mini golf?

Many toddlers enjoy pushing a ball with a small putter from around two or three. Formal scoring usually makes sense from about six or seven.

How long does a round of mini golf take with kids?

It varies by course and crowd, but allow extra time compared with an adults-only round. Nine holes is a comfortable target for young children.

Should we let kids win?

Rather than letting them win outright, level the field with shorter tees, stroke limits or team formats. Kids enjoy victories more when they feel earned.

Is mini golf suitable for children with limited mobility?

Many courses have accessible routes, and some are fully wheelchair accessible. Contact the specific course in advance to ask about paths, steps and putter options.

Play safe: swing low near people, pets and windows, and follow any posted course rules.
