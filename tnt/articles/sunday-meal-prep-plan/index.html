Nutrition & meal prep

The 90-Minute Sunday Meal Prep Plan (Step by Step)

By the Toned ’N Tasty team•Last updated September 23, 2026

Meal prep fails for most people in one of two ways: it takes the whole afternoon, or it produces five identical boxes of something you are sick of by Wednesday. This plan avoids both. It uses the oven and the stovetop at the same time, it cooks components rather than finished meals, and it takes about 90 minutes once you have done it a couple of times. The result is enough for four work lunches and four breakfasts, with flexibility built in.

Components, not meals

The big idea is to cook building blocks that can be combined in different ways during the week. One protein, one grain, two trays of vegetables and one or two sauces can become a burrito bowl on Monday, a grain salad on Tuesday and a wrap on Wednesday. It is the same work as cooking five identical meals, but it feels far less repetitive to eat.

Before you start

  • Empty the dishwasher and clear the counter. A clean start saves more time than any shortcut.
  • Take out every ingredient and all your containers.
  • Get two sheet pans, a large pot, a muffin tin, a cutting board and a sharp knife ready.
  • Start a bowl for scraps, so you aren't walking to the bin every minute.
  • Put on a podcast. This is surprisingly important for doing it every week.

The 90-minute timeline

The order matters because the oven is the bottleneck. Things that take longest go in first, and you chop and assemble while they cook.

Minute-by-minute plan (oven at 425°F / 220°C, then 350°F / 175°C)
TimeWhat you do
0:00Heat the oven to 425°F. Rinse and start the rice or quinoa on the stove.
0:05Peel and cube the sweet potatoes and slice the onion. Toss with oil and salt on tray 2.
0:15Tray 2 goes in the oven. Cut the broccoli into florets, toss with oil and salt on tray 1.
0:25Tray 1 goes in. Slice and season the chicken with the spice mix.
0:35Grains done: fluff and spread on a tray or large plate to cool quickly. Stir the vegetables.
0:45Vegetables out. Chicken in on a clean tray for about 20 minutes, until 165°F (74°C).
0:50Whisk the egg muffin mixture and fill the tin. Make the yogurt sauce. Chop the fresh salad.
1:05Chicken out. Lower the oven to 350°F and bake the egg cups for 20–24 minutes.
1:10Wash up while the egg cups bake. Start portioning the cooled components.
1:30Egg cups out to cool. Everything else is in the fridge. Done.

Your first attempt will probably take longer, and that is normal. By the third week, you will know your oven, your knife skills will be faster and you will stop checking the plan.

Packing for the week

Pack lunches in a way that keeps textures good. Put the grain and protein in the main container, and keep the sauce in a small separate pot. Leave the fresh element, such as cucumbers, tomatoes and herbs, in its own container and add it in the morning, because it goes soggy sitting against warm or saucy food. Everything cooked should be in the fridge within two hours of coming off the heat.

Food-safety guidance says most cooked leftovers are best eaten within three to four days. For a five-day work week, a simple solution is to freeze Thursday's and Friday's lunches and move them to the fridge the night before, or plan a quick mid-week top-up, such as a fresh batch of fajitas on Wednesday night.

Five lunches from the same components

  1. Burrito bowl: rice, chicken, roasted onion, salsa, a spoon of yogurt sauce.
  2. Warm grain salad: quinoa, sweet potato, broccoli, chicken, lemon-yogurt sauce.
  3. Wrap: whole-wheat tortilla, chicken, fresh salad, sauce, a little cheese.
  4. Stir-fry style: reheat rice, chicken and broccoli with a splash of soy sauce and chili crisp.
  5. Loaded sweet potato: sweet potato, chicken, black beans from the pantry, salsa.

Keeping it sustainable

Treat the plan as a starting template, not a rulebook. Rotate one component each week, such as a new sauce or a different vegetable, rather than changing everything at once. Keep a note on your phone of combinations you liked. Some weeks you will only have 45 minutes; on those weeks, cook just a protein and a grain and buy pre-cut vegetables. Doing some prep every week beats doing perfect prep occasionally.

Frequently asked questions

What containers should I use?

Any containers that seal well, stack neatly and can go in the microwave. Glass is sturdy and doesn't hold smells; lightweight plastic is easier to carry. Buy a set with matching lids to save frustration.

Can I prep for a whole week?

Cooked food is generally best eaten within three to four days of cooking, so freeze the later portions or do a small mid-week cook.

I get bored of the same food. Any tips?

Change the sauce. The same chicken and rice tastes completely different with salsa, peanut-lime sauce or tzatziki.

Do I need to reheat everything?

No. Grain salads and many bowls are good cold, as long as they have been kept refrigerated. Reheat cooked meats and rice until steaming if you prefer them warm.
