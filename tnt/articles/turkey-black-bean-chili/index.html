Big-Batch Turkey and Black Bean Chili

By the Toned ’N Tasty team•Last updated September 23, 2026

If you only learn one meal-prep recipe, make it a pot of chili. It uses cheap pantry staples, it tastes better on day two, it freezes beautifully, and a bowl is filling enough to count as a proper dinner. This version uses lean ground turkey and two kinds of beans for a lot of protein, plus a few flavour moves that stop turkey chili from tasting thin.

Why turkey chili sometimes tastes bland (and how to fix it)

Lean ground turkey has less fat than beef, and fat carries a lot of flavour. The fix is to build flavour in other ways. First, brown the meat properly: spread it out, leave it alone for a few minutes so it gets a crust, then break it up. Second, "bloom" the spices by frying them in the oil for a minute before the liquid goes in, which makes them taste deeper and less dusty. Third, add a little tomato paste and cook it until it darkens. Finally, finish with something bright, such as lime juice or a splash of vinegar, right before serving.

Turkey and Black Bean Chili

Serves 6Prep 15 minCook 45 minFreezes well
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • 2 lb (about 900 g) 93% lean ground turkey
  • 1 large onion, diced
  • 1 red bell pepper, diced
  • 1 jalapeño, seeded and minced (optional)
  • 4 cloves garlic, minced
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 2 tbsp chili powder
  • 2 tsp ground cumin
  • 1 tsp smoked paprika
  • 1 tsp dried oregano
  • 1 1/2 tsp kosher salt, plus more to taste
  • 1 can (28 oz / 794 g) crushed tomatoes
  • 1 can (15 oz) black beans, drained and rinsed
  • 1 can (15 oz) kidney or pinto beans, drained and rinsed
  • 1 cup (240 ml) low-sodium chicken broth or water
  • 1 tbsp lime juice or cider vinegar
  • Toppings: plain Greek yogurt, sliced green onion, cilantro, shredded cheese, avocado

Steps

  1. Heat the oil in a large heavy pot over medium-high heat. Add the turkey, press it into an even layer, and cook undisturbed for 4 minutes until browned underneath. Break it up and cook until no pink remains.
  2. Add the onion, bell pepper and jalapeño. Cook, stirring, for 5–6 minutes until softened.
  3. Stir in the garlic, tomato paste, chili powder, cumin, paprika, oregano and salt. Cook for 1–2 minutes, stirring, until the paste darkens and the spices smell toasty.
  4. Add the crushed tomatoes, both cans of beans and the broth. Scrape up any browned bits from the bottom of the pot.
  5. Bring to a simmer, then lower the heat, partially cover, and simmer for 30 minutes, stirring now and then. Add a splash more broth if it gets too thick.
  6. Stir in the lime juice, taste, and adjust the salt. Serve with your toppings.

Rough protein estimate: about 35 g per serving across six bowls, before toppings.

Slow cooker and pressure cooker versions

Slow cooker: Do steps 1 to 3 in a skillet (browning is still worth it), then scrape everything into the slow cooker with the tomatoes, beans and broth. Cook on low for 6–8 hours or high for 3–4 hours. Stir in the lime at the end.

Electric pressure cooker: Use the sauté function for steps 1 to 3. Add the broth first and scrape the bottom well so the machine doesn't give a burn warning, then add the beans, and pour the crushed tomatoes on top without stirring. Cook at high pressure for 10 minutes, then let the pressure release naturally for 10 minutes before opening.

Portioning and freezing

Chili is ideal for the freezer. The key is to cool it quickly and safely before it goes in. Divide it into shallow containers so it chills fast, get it into the fridge within two hours of cooking, and freeze what you won't eat within three to four days.

Ways to serve it all week

  • Classic bowl with Greek yogurt, green onion and a little cheese.
  • Over a baked potato or sweet potato for a heartier plate.
  • Chili mac: stir into cooked whole-wheat pasta.
  • Stuffed peppers: fill halved bell peppers, top with cheese, bake at 375°F (190°C) for about 25 minutes.
  • Breakfast: spoon warm chili over two fried eggs.

Make it your own

Swap the turkey for lean ground beef or chicken, or go meatless with a third can of beans and a cup of cooked lentils. For more smokiness, add one or two minced chipotle peppers in adobo. For more vegetables, stir in diced zucchini or a couple of handfuls of spinach during the last ten minutes. A square of dark chocolate or a teaspoon of cocoa powder adds depth that people notice but can rarely name.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to rinse canned beans?

Rinsing removes some of the starchy, salty liquid, which gives a cleaner flavour and lets you control the salt. It is not strictly necessary.

Can I use dried beans?

Yes. About 1 1/2 cups of cooked beans replaces each 15 oz can. Cook them fully before adding; undercooked kidney beans in particular should never be used.

My chili is too spicy. What now?

Stir in more crushed tomatoes or beans to dilute it, and serve with cooling Greek yogurt. Sugar or a little honey can also soften the heat.

Why is my turkey chili watery?

Simmer it uncovered for another 10 to 15 minutes, or mash a ladle of beans against the side of the pot to thicken it.
