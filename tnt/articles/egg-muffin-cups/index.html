Make-Ahead Egg Muffin Cups (That Don't Turn Rubbery)

By the Toned ’N Tasty team•Last updated September 23, 2026

Egg muffin cups are a mini frittata baked in a muffin tin. One batch gives you twelve portable breakfasts that reheat in under a minute. They are also a sneaky way to use up the odds and ends of vegetables left at the end of the week. The only real challenge is texture, because eggs baked the wrong way can come out spongy, watery or glued to the pan. Here is how to get them right.

The base recipe

Egg Muffin Cups (12 cups)

Makes 12Prep 15 minBake 20–24 minOven 350°F / 175°C
  • 10 large eggs
  • 1 cup (240 ml) liquid egg whites
  • 1/2 cup (about 115 g) cottage cheese
  • 1/2 tsp kosher salt and 1/4 tsp black pepper
  • 2 cups chopped fillings, cooked where noted below
  • 1/2 cup crumbled feta or shredded cheddar
  • Oil spray, or silicone/parchment muffin liners

Steps

  1. Heat the oven to 350°F (175°C). Grease a 12-cup muffin tin very well, or use silicone liners, which release far more easily than paper.
  2. Cook any watery vegetables first (see the table below). Let them cool a little and squeeze spinach dry in a towel.
  3. Whisk the eggs, egg whites, cottage cheese, salt and pepper in a large jug until smooth. A blender makes them fluffier, but a whisk is fine.
  4. Divide the fillings and cheese between the cups, filling each about one-third full.
  5. Pour the egg mixture over the top, filling each cup about three-quarters full. They puff up while baking.
  6. Bake for 20–24 minutes, until the centres are set and no longer wet when you press them gently. Egg dishes should reach 160°F (71°C).
  7. Cool in the tin for 5 minutes, then run a thin knife around each one and lift out. Cool completely on a rack before storing.

Rough protein estimate: about 9 g per cup, so three cups make roughly a 27 g breakfast.

Why egg cups turn rubbery or watery

Rubbery usually means overbaked. Eggs keep cooking from their own heat after they leave the oven, so pull them as soon as the centres are set. Lower temperatures, like 350°F, give you a wider margin than a hot oven.

Watery usually means raw, high-moisture vegetables. Mushrooms, zucchini, spinach, tomatoes and onions all release water as they bake, which pools at the bottom of the cup. Sauté them briefly first, or use them sparingly. Cottage cheese helps too: it adds protein and gives the eggs a softer, custard-like set.

Stuck to the pan means the tin wasn't greased enough. Eggs are famously sticky. Silicone liners or a silicone muffin pan solve this almost completely.

You don't have to pick one combination. Divide the tin into sections and make three or four flavours in the same batch.

Storing and reheating

  • Cool completely before packing, or condensation will make them soggy.
  • Line the container with a paper towel to catch moisture.
  • Refrigerate for up to 3–4 days.
  • To freeze, spread them on a tray until firm, then bag them; they are best used within a couple of months.
  • Reheat from the fridge in the microwave for 30–45 seconds, or from frozen for 60–90 seconds, wrapped in a damp paper towel, until hot all the way through.

Building a full breakfast around them

Two or three egg cups are the protein part of breakfast. Round it out with something that has fibre and some carbohydrate if you are training later in the day: a slice of whole-grain toast, a piece of fruit, or a small bowl of berries and yogurt. Wrapping two cups in a warm tortilla with salsa makes an easy handheld breakfast burrito for the car.

Frequently asked questions

Can I make them with only egg whites?

Yes, use about 2 1/2 cups of liquid egg whites. They will be leaner but a bit spongier; the cottage cheese and some cheese help the texture.

Can I skip the cottage cheese?

Yes. Replace it with 1/4 cup of milk, or just leave it out. The cups will be a little firmer.

How do I know they are done?

The tops should look set and spring back when pressed lightly, with no liquid egg in the centre. A thermometer should read 160°F (71°C).

Can I bake them in a mini muffin tin?

Yes. Start checking at about 12 minutes because smaller cups cook faster.
