Sesame Salmon Rice Bowls with Quick Pickled Cucumbers

By the Toned ’N Tasty team•Last updated September 23, 2026

A good bowl has a bit of everything: something rich, something fresh, something crunchy and something to soak up the sauce. This salmon bowl ticks all of those boxes with very little work. The salmon roasts in the oven while you make the rice and a two-minute cucumber pickle, and the sticky sesame glaze doubles as the dressing.

The recipe

Sesame Salmon Rice Bowls

Serves 4Prep 15 minCook 15 minOven 400°F / 200°C

Quick pickled cucumbers

  • 1 large cucumber (or 3 small ones), thinly sliced
  • 1/4 cup (60 ml) rice vinegar
  • 1 tsp sugar and 1/2 tsp salt

Glaze

  • 3 tbsp low-sodium soy sauce or tamari
  • 1 tbsp honey or maple syrup
  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame oil
  • 1 tbsp rice vinegar
  • 1 tsp grated fresh ginger and 1 clove garlic, grated
  • 1 tsp sriracha or chili crisp (optional)

Bowls

  • 4 salmon fillets, about 5–6 oz (140–170 g) each
  • 3 cups cooked rice (white, brown or a mix)
  • 1 1/2 cups frozen shelled edamame
  • 2 carrots, grated or cut into matchsticks
  • 2 green onions, sliced; 1 tbsp sesame seeds
  • Optional: sliced avocado, nori strips, shredded red cabbage

Steps

  1. Heat the oven to 400°F (200°C) and line a baking sheet with parchment.
  2. Pickles first: toss the cucumber with the vinegar, sugar and salt in a bowl and set aside. They are ready in 15 minutes and improve as they sit.
  3. Whisk the glaze ingredients together. Pour about a third into a small bowl to use as a dressing later; this portion never touches raw fish.
  4. Pat the salmon dry, place it skin-side down on the sheet, and brush with the remaining glaze.
  5. Roast for 10–14 minutes, depending on thickness, until the salmon flakes easily and reaches 145°F (63°C) in the thickest part. Brush with any leftover glaze halfway through.
  6. Meanwhile, cook the edamame according to the packet (usually 3–5 minutes in boiling water or the microwave) and drain.
  7. Build each bowl: rice, salmon, edamame, carrots and drained pickles. Drizzle with the reserved dressing and finish with green onion and sesame seeds.

Rough protein estimate: around 40 g per bowl, depending on fillet size.

Buying salmon without overthinking it

You don't need the most expensive fillet in the case for a glazed bowl. Frozen salmon, sold in individually wrapped portions, is often a good value and is usually frozen soon after processing. Thaw it overnight in the fridge, or in its sealed packaging in a bowl of cold water for about an hour. Skin-on fillets hold together better during roasting, and the skin slides off easily once cooked if you don't want to eat it.

Whether you choose wild or farmed salmon is a question of taste, budget and personal priorities. Wild salmon tends to be leaner and firmer, so it can dry out a minute or two sooner; check it early.

Meal prepping salmon bowls

Cooked salmon keeps for three to four days in the fridge, so these bowls prep well if you plan to eat them within a few days. Pack the rice and salmon together, and keep the pickles and dressing in small separate containers so the rice doesn't get soggy. Many people enjoy the salmon cold, flaked over the rice like a poke-style bowl, which also avoids the fishy smell of reheating it in a shared office microwave. If you do reheat it, use a lower power setting and cover it loosely so it doesn't dry out.

A note on rice: cooked rice should be cooled and refrigerated promptly, ideally within an hour or two, because spores of a bacterium called Bacillus cereus can survive cooking and multiply if rice sits warm for a long time. Spreading it on a tray helps it cool quickly.

Variations

  • Tofu: Press extra-firm tofu, cut it into cubes, toss with a tablespoon of cornstarch, and roast at 425°F (220°C) for 25 minutes before tossing it in the glaze.
  • Chicken thighs: Roast glazed boneless thighs for 18–22 minutes to 165°F (74°C).
  • Shrimp: Toss peeled shrimp in the glaze and roast for 6–8 minutes.
  • Greens instead of rice: Swap half the rice for shredded cabbage or baby spinach for a lighter bowl.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use the leftover glaze that touched the raw salmon?

Not as a dressing. That is why the recipe sets aside a portion before brushing the fish. Any glaze that touched raw fish should be cooked on the salmon or thrown away.

Can I air-fry the salmon?

Yes. Air-fry at about 390°F (200°C) for 7 to 10 minutes, depending on thickness. Line the basket with parchment because honey glazes can scorch.

How long do quick pickles keep?

In a covered jar in the fridge, they stay crisp for several days, though they soften gradually.

Is brown rice better here?

Either works. Brown rice adds some fibre and a nuttier bite; white rice is softer and soaks up the sauce. Use whichever you enjoy.
