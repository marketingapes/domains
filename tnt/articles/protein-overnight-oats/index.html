Protein Overnight Oats: One Base Recipe, Three Flavours

By the Toned ’N Tasty team•Last updated September 23, 2026

Breakfast is the meal where protein most often goes missing. Toast, cereal and a coffee can add up to very little. Overnight oats fix that with almost no morning effort: you stir a jar together before bed, and it is ready to eat cold, straight from the fridge. Adding Greek yogurt turns a mostly-carb breakfast into one with a respectable amount of protein, and it makes the texture creamier too.

The base recipe

Think of this as a ratio rather than a rigid recipe. Once you know it, you can scale it up to four jars on a Sunday or make a single portion in a mug.

Greek Yogurt Overnight Oats (1 jar)

Serves 1Prep 5 minChill 6+ hours
  • 1/2 cup (40 g) old-fashioned rolled oats
  • 3/4 cup (170 g) plain nonfat or 2% Greek yogurt
  • 1/2 cup (120 ml) milk of your choice
  • 1 tbsp chia seeds
  • 1–2 tsp maple syrup or honey (optional)
  • Pinch of salt and 1/4 tsp vanilla extract

Steps

  1. Add the oats, chia and salt to a jar or container that holds at least 16 oz (about 500 ml).
  2. Add the yogurt, milk, sweetener and vanilla, then stir well, scraping the bottom so no dry oats hide there.
  3. Stir in your flavour add-ins (see below), cover and refrigerate for at least 6 hours or overnight.
  4. In the morning, stir, loosen with a splash of milk if it is too thick, and add fresh toppings.

Rough protein estimate: about 25–30 g per jar with nonfat Greek yogurt and dairy milk. Plant milks and plant yogurts vary widely, so check the labels.

Three flavours to rotate

The base is mild on purpose. Pick one of these and you will not get bored for a while.

1. Peanut butter and banana

Stir in 1 tablespoon of peanut butter (or powdered peanut butter mixed with a little water for fewer calories) and half a mashed banana before chilling. Top with the other half of the banana, sliced, and a few chopped peanuts in the morning. The banana sweetens it enough that many people skip the syrup.

2. Berry cheesecake

Add 1/4 teaspoon of lemon zest and a small handful of frozen mixed berries to the jar. The berries thaw overnight and bleed into the oats, giving you a pink, jammy swirl. For a "crust", sprinkle a crushed graham cracker or a spoon of granola on top just before eating so it stays crunchy.

3. Apple pie

Stir in 1/2 teaspoon cinnamon, a pinch of nutmeg and 1/3 cup of grated apple. Grating instead of chopping means the apple softens and flavours the whole jar. Top with a few toasted walnuts or pecans.

Texture troubleshooting

Quick fixes for common overnight oat problems
ProblemLikely causeFix
Thick like pasteChia and oats absorbed all the liquidStir in 2–3 tbsp milk in the morning, or use less chia next time
SoupyToo much milk or a runny yogurtAdd an extra tablespoon of oats or chia and give it another hour
Chalky tasteProtein powder added without enough liquidAdd 1/4 cup extra milk per scoop, or skip powder and rely on yogurt
Gluey, mushyQuick oats instead of rolled oatsUse old-fashioned rolled oats; save quick oats for cooked porridge
Too sourPlain Greek yogurt is tangyAdd mashed fruit or a little more sweetener; 2% yogurt tastes milder

Batch prep and storage

Overnight oats keep well in the fridge for about four days, so you can make Monday to Thursday in one go. Keep the crunchy toppings (nuts, granola, seeds) in a separate small container and add them the morning you eat each jar, otherwise they turn soft. Fresh fruit like sliced banana is also best added on the day.

Wide-mouth jars are easiest to eat from and to wash. If you are taking breakfast to work, a jar with a screw lid is less likely to leak than a snap-top container.

  • Line up four jars and add dry ingredients to all of them first.
  • Add yogurt and milk, then stir each jar thoroughly.
  • Add a different flavour to each jar so the week has variety.
  • Label the lids with the flavour and the day you made them.
  • Pack a small bag of toppings for each day.

Can I add protein powder?

You can, but it changes the recipe. Protein powder absorbs a lot of liquid and can make oats taste dusty. If you want to use it, start with half a scoop and add an extra 1/4 cup of milk, then adjust. Many people find the Greek-yogurt version already gets breakfast into a comfortable range without a powder, which is also cheaper.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to eat overnight oats cold?

No. You can microwave a jar (transfer it to a microwave-safe bowl if the lid is metal) for about a minute, stirring halfway. Add a splash of milk because it thickens as it warms.

Can I use steel-cut oats?

Steel-cut oats stay very chewy when soaked rather than cooked. Rolled oats give the classic soft texture; if you love steel-cut, give them a full 12 hours and expect more bite.

Is chia necessary?

No. Chia thickens the mixture and adds a little fibre, but you can leave it out and use slightly less milk.

How long do they keep?

About four days in the fridge in a sealed container. If the jar smells sour beyond the normal yogurt tang, or looks off, throw it out.
