01 / The story

I didn’t start
with AI.

I started with marketing. Over the last 20 years, working with 40 different agencies, I kept coming back to the same problem: the ad account tells one story, the intake team tells another, and the business outcome lives somewhere else.

Every agency had tools, accounts and reports. The problem was the space between them. Ads, websites, calls and the CRM were often treated as separate jobs. I wanted them working toward the same business outcome: what happened after the lead came in, and what should we change because of it?

“I wanted the marketing to learn from the business, not just from the advertising platform.”

Evolution Engine is my answer to that problem. AI hasn’t replaced the experience. It helps turn that experience into working pages, connected accounts, workflows and reporting. The goal is to spend less time passing information between tools and more time making the next useful decision.

02 / The breakthrough

Follow the money.
Then the outcome.

An inexpensive lead is not automatically a profitable lead. Connect spend to inquiries, inquiries to follow-up, and follow-up to the firm’s actual outcomes. Then use that evidence to choose the next change.

Spend → inquiries → AI follow-up → firm outcomes → optimization
01 · MARKETING

Where did the opportunity come from?

Campaigns, channels, creative, spend and submissions. Social is a way to help people discover the offer; a firm does not have to become an influencer to participate. Keep the source attached to the inquiry.

02 · AI INTAKE

What happened next?

Answer the phone. Respond to the inquiry. Follow up within consent, screen against agreed criteria and hand off clearly. Automation supports the work; people remain responsible for criteria and exceptions.

03 · FIRM FEEDBACK

Was it useful to the firm?

Separate receipt from contact, qualification and case outcome. Feed permitted, verified signals into measurement and optimization where supported.

THE COMMERCE MINDSET

Lead generation needs
an outcome, too.

Ecommerce connects an ad to a visit, a checkout and an order. Lead generation should have that same discipline, even though a legal matter is not an online purchase.

Connect the inquiry to the call, the handoff and the firm’s decision. A submitted form is not a signed client. A CRM receipt is not a retained case. The feedback is what lets us understand which opportunities were valuable.

The loop: advertise, respond, learn what happened, then improve the next decision. That is the system, not a promise that every channel or campaign will work.

Programmable AI intake

Different campaigns.
Different questions.

The next conversation should fit the campaign, not a generic script.

The system’s AI-assisted pre-qualification approach uses campaign-specific questions and firm-approved screening criteria. The aim is to gather the right information, identify what needs a human review and make the handoff easier to act on.

Define the criteria. Shape the conversation. Review the result. Questions, follow-up paths and escalation rules can be designed for each campaign, then tested before use. A callback or firm handoff belongs in that flow when the person’s consent and the campaign’s routing support it.

Pre-qualification is an intake aid, not a legal determination, a guarantee that someone qualifies or proof of a signed case. The firm makes the legal and acceptance decisions. Each bot’s questions, consent handling, callback path and destination need verification for its campaign.

From campaign to system

The LA County
learning moment.

My work on the LA County sexual-abuse campaign with Phillips helped sharpen the question: what happens after we deliver the inquiry?

In my experience across repeated campaigns, getting an inquiry into the firm’s system was only part of the job. When intake feedback arrived slowly or lived in a separate report, I could see activity without having the full outcome in time to guide the next decision.

That is the proof-of-concept story I want to discuss: not a claim that a form submission became a case, but a practical reason to connect advertising, intake and firm feedback. The opportunity is a repeatable process that learns from what the firm actually reports.

One strategy. Different jobs.

Connect the channels.
Don’t treat them all the same.

The aim is not to buy every channel blindly. It is to give each channel a purpose, test its contribution and adjust with evidence.

Discovery and demand

Facebook, Instagram and TikTok can introduce an offer and invite an inquiry. Google Search can meet expressed intent. Performance Max can test delivery across Google inventory. OpenAI ads are another channel to evaluate where account access, formats and reporting support the campaign.

AI helps prepare creative variations, compare performance and recommend the next test. The campaign’s audience, consent requirements and economics determine where we actually invest.

Measurement and feedback

Server-side measurement connects permitted events to the right campaign, with consistent event IDs, deduplication and delivery logs. It complements browser tracking; it does not make incomplete or restricted data safe to send.

Keep sensitive intake answers and case details in the operational system, not advertising pixels. Hashing an email or phone number is not permission to share it. Send only supported, permitted signals and verify platform receipt before relying on them.

Less waste, better decisions

Use appropriate exclusions to avoid redundant acquisition messaging, but only where consent, platform rules and the sensitivity of the campaign allow. A legal inquiry is not automatically a reusable advertising audience.

Keep stop-contact instructions enforced in follow-up. Compare cost per qualified opportunity and confirmed business outcomes, not just cheap clicks.

A controlled iteration loop

Observe → test → measure → decide. Change a defined variable, keep the comparison meaningful and give the data enough time to mature. Real-time feedback means faster visibility, not a promise that ad platforms learn instantly.

Scale when the evidence supports it. ROI requires revenue and costs, and improvement is something to measure, not assume.

06 / Why it matters

The goal is profit.
The method is evidence.

The goal is not more clicks, more tools or more AI. It is spending intelligently on opportunities the firm can turn into business. AI helps execute and analyze the work; the results still have to justify the cost.

Start with one campaign and an agreed definition of success. Track spend, inquiries, qualified opportunities and firm outcomes. Compare cost per qualified opportunity, and cost per retained case when confirmed. Revenue and delivery costs are needed before we can call it ROI or profit.
