Price Matching and Price Adjustments: How to Ask, and When It Works

By The Discount Deal Me team · Last updated September 23, 2026 · 4 min read

Price matching means a store agrees to sell you an item at a lower price you found at a competitor. A price adjustment (sometimes called price protection) means a store refunds the difference if its own price drops shortly after you buy. Both can save real money on larger purchases, and both come with conditions that vary widely from store to store.

Many retailers have scaled back or changed these policies over the years, so the only reliable source is the store's current written policy. This guide explains what to look for and how to ask.

Price matching vs price adjustment

The two policies at a glance
Price matchPrice adjustment
What it comparesThis store vs a competitorThis store's price now vs what you paid
When you askUsually before or at purchaseAfter purchase, within a set window
Typical proofCompetitor's live listing, same item, in stockYour receipt or order number
Common exclusionsMarketplace sellers, membership prices, limited-time or clearance dealsClearance, doorbusters, limited-quantity events

Conditions stores commonly attach

Price-match policies usually include a list of rules. Reading them before you ask saves time for you and the staff member. Typical conditions include:

  • Identical item. Same brand, model number, size, colour and condition. New versus refurbished does not match.
  • Named competitors only. Some stores match only a specified list of retailers, or only local stores.
  • Sold by the retailer, not a marketplace seller. Third-party listings on large marketplaces are often excluded.
  • In stock and available. The competitor must have it available at that price now, often including shipping in the comparison.
  • Excluded promotions. Membership-only prices, bundle deals, rebates, "buy one get one" offers and time-limited flash sales are commonly excluded.
  • Quantity limits. Many policies limit how many units they will match per customer.

How to ask for a price match

  1. Read the policy first. Find it on the store's website, often in the help or FAQ section, and check your item qualifies.
  2. Get the evidence ready. Open the competitor's live listing on your phone showing the price, the model number and availability. A screenshot helps but many stores need to verify live.
  3. Ask at the right place. In store, that is usually at the register or customer service. Online, many stores handle it through chat before you order.
  4. Be specific and polite. "Your policy says you match this retailer's price on identical items. Here's the same model in stock for less. Could you match it?"
  5. Accept a no gracefully. Staff may have limited discretion. If the gap is worth it, you can simply buy from the cheaper store.

Getting a price adjustment after you buy

If a store offers price adjustments, the process is usually simpler: within the stated window, show your receipt or order number and the current lower price, and the store refunds the difference to your original payment method or as store credit. Windows are often short, commonly a couple of weeks, and some stores exclude big sale events from adjustments entirely.

This is where price tracking pays off. If you set an alert on items you have just bought, you will know if the price drops while you are still inside the window.

If a store does not offer adjustments but the item is still within its return window, some shoppers return the item and rebuy it at the lower price. Check the return policy first: restocking fees, return shipping or open-box rules can make this a losing move, and it creates work for the store, so reserve it for meaningful differences.

Is it worth the effort?

For small purchases, usually not. For appliances, electronics, furniture and other larger items, even a modest percentage difference can be worth a five-minute conversation. The most valuable part of the process is often the policy check itself: once you know which of your regular stores match and adjust, you can buy with more confidence and less second-guessing.

Keeping records that make matching easy

Stores decide price-match and adjustment requests on evidence, so a little record-keeping goes a long way. Keep digital receipts in one email folder, note the model number of anything expensive you buy, and screenshot competitor prices with the date and time visible. If you are asking in store, have the competitor page loaded before you reach the counter, because slow mobile data can turn a quick request into an awkward wait. For online orders, many retailers handle adjustments through their chat support, so copy the order number and the link to the current lower price into the chat in your first message. The clearer the request, the more likely it is to be approved without back-and-forth, and the less time you spend on a saving that may only be a few dollars.

Finally, a note on courtesy: the person at the counter did not write the policy. A friendly, well-prepared request usually goes further than an argument, and when the answer is no, the cheaper store is still there.

Frequently asked questions

Do all stores price match?

No. Some have formal policies, some match informally at a manager's discretion, and some do not match at all. Policies also change, so check the current version before you shop.

Can I price match an online price in a physical store?

Some stores allow it if the online retailer is on their approved list and the item is identical and in stock. Others match only local competitors.

What is price protection on credit cards?

Some cards once offered refunds when an item's price dropped after purchase, but many issuers have discontinued it. Check your own card's current benefits guide.

Will a store match its own website price in store?

Many do, but not all, and some exclude online-only promotions. It is worth asking at the register with the listing open.
