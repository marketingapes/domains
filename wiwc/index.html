What is working capital, really?

It is the money that keeps your doors open between paying suppliers and getting paid by customers. We explain how it works, how to measure it, and what to do when it runs thin, in plain English with worked examples.

Working capital = Current assets − Current liabilities

Why this site exists

Search for "working capital" and almost every result is written by someone who wants to lend it to you. The explanation tends to stop exactly where the sales pitch starts. We wanted the opposite: a calm, independent place that explains how money actually moves through a small business, so owners can make their own decisions and ask better questions of lenders, accountants and suppliers.

Everything here is educational. We use round, made-up numbers in our examples so the arithmetic is easy to follow, and we show our working. We do not rank lenders, take applications or sell financial products, and nothing on this site is personal financial advice.

Five terms in thirty seconds

TermPlain-English meaning
Current assetsThings that will turn into cash within about a year: cash, customer invoices, stock, prepaid bills.
Current liabilitiesBills you must pay within about a year: suppliers, card balances, taxes, loan repayments due soon.
Current ratioCurrent assets divided by current liabilities. How many dollars of short-term resources per dollar owed.
Cash conversion cycleDays between paying for stock and collecting from the customer who bought it.
Trade creditThe time suppliers give you to pay. Usually the cheapest financing a business has.
