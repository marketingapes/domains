The quote process

How to Compare Life Insurance Quotes Without Getting Lost

By the Smart Life Insurance Quote team · Last updated 23 September 2026

Educational information, not professional advice. Policies and rules vary by insurer and state; check details with a licensed professional.

Getting a life insurance quote is easy. Comparing quotes is where people get lost, because two numbers that look similar can describe very different policies. This guide explains what a quote really represents and how to line options up so you are comparing like with like.

First, what a quote is (and is not)

An online or initial quote is an estimate. It assumes a rating class, usually based on the basic information you entered. The real price is only set after underwriting, which may place you in a different class. That is why a quote that looks cheapest at the start can end up more expensive once your actual health, history and lifestyle are assessed. Treat initial quotes as a way to compare products and insurers, not as final prices.

What you will usually be asked

To produce a meaningful quote, an insurer or agent typically asks for your date of birth, sex, state of residence, height and weight, nicotine use, a broad picture of your health, the amount of cover and the term you want. Having your DIME estimate ready, and knowing when your longest need ends, makes the conversation much quicker.

A six-step fair comparison

  1. Match the face amount. Compare quotes for exactly the same death benefit.
  2. Match the term or policy type. A 20-year term and a 30-year term are different products; so are term and permanent.
  3. Match the assumed rating class. Ask each source which class it assumed. A preferred quote from one insurer versus a standard quote from another tells you little.
  4. Match the riders. Note which riders are included and which are extra, and price them the same way across quotes.
  5. Check the conversion privilege. How long can you convert, and into which products?
  6. Check the insurer. Look at financial strength ratings and the company's claims reputation.

Checking the insurer behind the quote

A life insurance policy is a long promise, so the company's ability to keep it matters. Independent rating agencies such as AM Best, S&P, Moody's and Fitch publish financial strength ratings for insurers. You can also check your state insurance department for the company's licence status and complaint information, and the NAIC's consumer tools for complaint data. None of these guarantee future performance, but together they give a reasonable picture.

Who you buy through

Whoever you speak to, it is reasonable to ask how they are paid. Agents are commonly paid a commission by the insurer, which is built into the premium. That is normal, but knowing it helps you judge advice, especially on permanent products where commissions tend to be larger. You can confirm an agent is licensed through your state insurance department.

Traps to watch for

  • Comparing monthly and annual prices. Paying monthly often costs more over a year than paying annually. Compare on the same basis.
  • Replacing an existing policy. Do not cancel current cover until a new policy is issued and in force. You may also lose benefits of the old policy, such as passing its contestability period.
  • Illustrations presented as promises. For permanent products, ask for guaranteed values alongside projected values.
  • Pressure to decide today. A good policy will still be available tomorrow; your age is the only thing that changes quickly.

After you apply: the free look period

Once a policy is delivered, most states give you a free look period, often somewhere between 10 and 30 days depending on the state and policy, during which you can cancel for a refund of premiums paid. Use that time to read the contract, confirm the beneficiary details, check the riders and make sure the policy matches what you were quoted.

Frequently asked questions

Why did my final price differ from the quote?

Initial quotes assume a rating class. Underwriting may place you in a different class once your health and history are reviewed.

Does getting quotes affect my credit score?

Getting a quote does not usually involve a hard credit inquiry. The application process may involve checks you authorise, so read the consent forms.

Is buying online cheaper than through an agent?

Not necessarily. Premiums for the same policy are often the same regardless of channel. The difference is mostly in how much help you get.

How many quotes should I compare?

Enough to be confident you are seeing a fair range. Many people compare a few insurers for the same amount, term and class.
