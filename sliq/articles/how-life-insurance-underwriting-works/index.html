Underwriting

How Life Insurance Underwriting Works, Step by Step

By the Smart Life Insurance Quote team · Last updated 23 September 2026

Educational information, not professional advice. Policies and rules vary by insurer and state; check details with a licensed professional.

Underwriting is the insurer's process of deciding whether to offer you a policy, and on what terms. It is why two people of the same age can be quoted very different prices. Understanding the process takes some of the mystery out of the wait, and helps you avoid the most common causes of delays.

What underwriters are trying to work out

At heart, an underwriter is estimating how likely it is that the insurer will pay a claim during the policy, and whether the amount of cover you have asked for makes sense for your situation. They are also checking that the application is complete and accurate. The answer is expressed as a rating class, which sets the premium, or occasionally as a postponement or decline.

The information they gather

  • Your application. Age, sex, height and weight, medical history, medications, family history, occupation, finances, travel and hobbies.
  • Exam and lab results, if a paramedical exam was required.
  • Prescription history. Insurers commonly check pharmacy databases, with your authorisation, to see medications you have been prescribed.
  • MIB check. MIB is a membership organisation that lets insurers see coded information from previous life and health insurance applications. It helps catch errors and omissions.
  • Driving record. Serious violations or a license suspension can affect your class.
  • Attending physician statement. For some applicants, the insurer requests records from your doctor. This is the most common cause of slow applications.
  • Financial information, to confirm the amount of cover is reasonable compared with income and net worth.

Some risks, such as certain dangerous hobbies or occupations, are priced with a flat extra: an additional charge per thousand of cover, sometimes for a limited number of years. Others may lead to an exclusion or a postponement until a condition has been stable for a while.

How long it takes

Accelerated underwriting can produce a decision within days, sometimes faster, for applicants who fit the insurer's criteria. Traditional, fully underwritten applications commonly take several weeks, and longer if doctors' records are slow to arrive. You can often speed things up by listing doctors and dates accurately, completing the exam promptly and answering follow-up questions quickly.

Why honesty is non-negotiable

It can be tempting to round your weight down or forget an old diagnosis. Don't. Prescription databases and MIB mean many omissions are found during underwriting anyway. If one is not found and a claim is made during the contestability period (commonly the first two years), the insurer can investigate and may deny the claim or adjust the payout if it finds a material misrepresentation. That risk falls on your family at the worst possible moment.

If the offer is not what you hoped

Options after a disappointing offer

  • Ask which factors drove the rating class; you are entitled to understand the decision.
  • Check the records for errors, including your MIB file, which you can request.
  • If a temporary issue affected results, ask whether the insurer will reconsider later.
  • Consider applying elsewhere: insurers weigh some conditions differently.
  • Ask whether a lower amount or shorter term changes the outcome.

An independent agent or broker who works with several insurers can sometimes suggest which companies tend to be more comfortable with a particular condition before you apply, which saves time and avoids multiple declines on your record.

Frequently asked questions

What is MIB in life insurance?

MIB is an organisation that keeps coded information from past insurance applications so insurers can spot errors or omissions. You can request a copy of your own file.

Does a table rating mean I was declined?

No. It means you were offered cover at a higher premium than standard because of higher expected risk.

Can I reapply if my health improves?

Yes. Many people reapply or ask for reconsideration after losing weight, quitting nicotine or stabilising a condition, although insurers set their own waiting periods.

Is vaping treated like smoking?

Many insurers treat any nicotine use, including vaping, as tobacco use for rating purposes. Check each insurer's definition.
