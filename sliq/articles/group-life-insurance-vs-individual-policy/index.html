Group Life Insurance at Work vs an Individual Policy

By the Smart Life Insurance Quote team · Last updated 23 September 2026

Educational information, not professional advice. Policies and rules vary by insurer and state; check details with a licensed professional.

Many employers offer life insurance as a benefit, and it is often the first cover people ever have. It is convenient and sometimes free. But workplace cover has limits that are easy to miss until you change jobs. This guide compares group life with a policy you own yourself, and explains how to decide whether you need both.

How group life usually works

Group life is a single master policy that the employer (or a union or association) holds, covering its eligible members. Coverage often comes in two layers. Basic cover is commonly paid for by the employer and set at a flat amount or a multiple of salary, such as one or two times pay. Supplemental or voluntary cover lets you buy more, usually through payroll deductions, and may also be offered for a spouse and children.

Enrolment is often easy. Basic cover may need no health questions, and supplemental cover up to a certain level may be guaranteed issue when you first become eligible. Above that level, or if you enrol later, you may need to answer health questions.

Group vs individual at a glance

The big catch: what happens when you leave

Most group cover ends when your employment ends, whether you resign, retire, are laid off or become too ill to work. Many plans offer two escape routes. Conversion lets you turn group cover into an individual permanent policy without proving your health, but it usually must be requested within a short window, often around 31 days, and converted policies can be expensive. Portability, where offered, lets you keep term cover at group-style rates for a while after leaving. Both are valuable if your health has changed, but neither is guaranteed to be cheap. Check your plan documents before you need them.

A tax detail worth knowing

In the United States, employer-paid group term life above 50,000 of coverage creates imputed income: the cost of the excess cover, calculated using an IRS table, is added to your taxable wages. It usually is not a large amount, but it explains a mysterious line on some payslips. Supplemental cover that you pay for with after-tax money generally does not create imputed income. Ask your HR or payroll team if you are unsure how it applies to you.

When workplace cover is probably not enough

  • You have dependants and the group amount falls well below a DIME estimate.
  • You might change jobs, go freelance or start a business in the coming years.
  • You want level premiums for a long term, rather than rates that rise in age bands.
  • You are in good health now and would like to lock that in with an individual policy.
  • Your spouse relies on spousal cover through your job, which would end with it.

Using both together

For many people the sensible approach is to treat free basic group cover as a bonus rather than the foundation. Buy an individual term policy sized for the core need, then use group cover for extra protection while you are employed there. When comparing the price of supplemental group cover with an individual policy, compare over several years, since group rates often step up at certain ages.

Questions to ask HR during enrolment

  • How much basic cover do I have, and who pays for it?
  • What is the guaranteed issue limit for supplemental cover?
  • How do supplemental rates change with age?
  • Is there a conversion or portability option, and what is the deadline?
  • Who is currently listed as my beneficiary?

Frequently asked questions

Does my work life insurance follow me to a new job?

Usually not. It typically ends when you leave, though you may be able to convert or port it within a short deadline.

Is employer life insurance enough?

It can be a useful base, but it is often a small multiple of salary and tied to your job. Many families with dependants need more.

Why is there imputed income on my payslip?

In the US, employer-paid group term cover above 50,000 creates taxable imputed income based on an IRS table.

Should I buy supplemental life at work or an individual policy?

Compare costs over several years and consider portability. Individual term policies often have level premiums, while supplemental group rates may rise with age.
