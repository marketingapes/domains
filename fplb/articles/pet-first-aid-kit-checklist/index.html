Build a Pet First-Aid Kit: A Checklist for Dogs and Cats

By the For Pets Like Blue team · Updated September 23, 2026 · 4 min read

Talk to your vet. This guide is general information for healthy pets, not veterinary advice. Every animal is different — if you are worried about your pet’s health, or anything here does not match what you are seeing, contact your vet.

Nobody likes to think about their pet getting hurt, but small emergencies happen: a torn nail on a walk, a cut paw pad on a beach, a bee sting in the garden. A well-stocked pet first-aid kit, plus a few phone numbers saved in your phone, helps you stay calm and act quickly while you get veterinary advice. This article explains what to include and how to use it sensibly. It is not a substitute for training or for your vet.

Your vet is the first-aid plan

First aid for pets is about keeping an animal as safe and comfortable as possible until they can be seen by a vet. It is not about treating serious problems at home. Before you need it, save these numbers in your phone and write them inside the kit lid:

  • Your regular vet practice, including its out-of-hours arrangements
  • The nearest 24-hour emergency vet
  • An animal poison helpline for your country, if one exists

Also note the route to the emergency vet. In a real emergency, phone ahead if you can so the team is ready for you.

The first-aid kit checklist

  • Your pet’s vaccination record, medication list and microchip number
  • Non-stick wound dressings (pads)
  • Conforming bandage and self-adhesive cohesive bandage (the type that sticks to itself, not to fur)
  • Adhesive tape
  • Sterile saline for rinsing wounds and eyes
  • Blunt-ended scissors
  • Tweezers and a tick remover
  • Disposable gloves
  • A clean towel or small blanket — useful for warmth, carrying, or gently wrapping a cat
  • A digital thermometer and lubricant, if your vet has shown you how to use it
  • Styptic powder for minor nail bleeds
  • A spare leash and a soft muzzle that fits your dog
  • A foil blanket and a torch
  • Collapsible water bowl and a bottle of water
  • A pen and notepad to record times and symptoms

Leave out human medications. Many common human medicines, including some painkillers and antihistamines, can be harmful to pets or need very different doses. Only give medication your vet has prescribed or advised for that situation.

Why a muzzle belongs in the kit

Even the gentlest pet may bite when in pain or frightened. A well-fitting basket or soft muzzle for dogs helps keep everyone safe while you handle an injury. Practise putting it on during calm times with treats so it is not a surprise. Do not muzzle a pet that is vomiting or struggling to breathe. For cats, wrapping gently in a towel with the head exposed can help you hold them safely.

Situations that need an urgent vet call

Call your vet or emergency vet straight away if your pet:

Situations that need urgent veterinary help
SignWhat to do while you call
Difficulty breathing or blue/grey gumsKeep them calm and still; go straight in
Bleeding that does not slow with firm pressureHold a clean pad firmly on the wound
Collapse, seizures or sudden weaknessMove objects away, keep them quiet, time any seizure
Suspected poisoningTake the packaging with you; do not make them vomit unless a vet tells you to
Swollen, hard belly with retching and no vomit (dogs)Go to a vet immediately — this can be bloat
Male cat straining with little or no urineGo to a vet immediately
Heatstroke signs: heavy panting, drooling, wobblinessMove to shade, cool with cool (not ice-cold) water, travel to vet
Hit by a car or a fall, even if they seem fineSupport the body on a flat surface or blanket and transport carefully

Minor issues you may manage with advice

Some smaller problems can often be handled at home after checking with your vet, such as a short nail bleed or a tick. For a small cut, you might rinse with saline and cover lightly while you ring for advice. Remove ticks with a tick remover by twisting gently, without squeezing the body. If a small wound is deep, looks dirty, was caused by a bite or keeps bleeding, it needs a vet.

A pet first-aid course can build real confidence. Some vet practices, charities and training providers run them in person or online.

Keep the kit ready

Store the kit somewhere everyone in the house knows, and take a smaller version on walks, hikes and trips. Check it every few months: replace used items, check expiry dates on saline and other supplies, and update your pet’s records.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use human first-aid supplies on my pet?

Many dressings, bandages and saline are fine for pets, but do not use human medicines, creams or antiseptics without checking with your vet, as some are harmful to animals.

Should I make my dog vomit if they ate something toxic?

Only if a vet or poison helpline tells you to. Some substances cause more damage coming back up, and home methods can be dangerous. Call for advice straight away.

Do cats need a different first-aid kit?

The basics are the same. A towel for safe handling and a sturdy carrier are especially important for cats, and a muzzle is less useful.
