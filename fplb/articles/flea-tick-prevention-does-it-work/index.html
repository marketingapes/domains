Does Your Dog’s Flea and Tick Prevention Actually Work? A Yes-or-No Check

By Blue’s family · Updated October 13, 2026 · 6 min read

Not veterinary advice. This page is general information. Your vet picks the product and the dose for your dog. Talk to them before you start, stop or switch any medication.

Blue is a big yellow Lab on three legs. He lost his front right leg after a blood infection put him on life support. We are not saying flea and tick prevention would have changed that. We are saying that after that week, we stopped being casual about the everyday stuff. Prevention in our house is routine, like dinner.

This page is not a review. We do not rate products out of five or tell you which brand “feels” best. We ask one question about whatever your dog is on right now: does it help the dog, yes or no? Then we help you not overpay for the one your vet picked.

The yes-or-no check

Run through this with your dog’s current product. If you answer “no” to any line, that is a conversation for your vet, not a reason to panic.

Is your dog getting every dose on time?The product has a fair chance to work.Gaps are the most common reason prevention “fails.” Set a phone reminder before blaming the product.
Is the dose matched to your dog’s current weight?Good. Weight bands matter.Dogs gain and lose weight. Ask your vet to confirm the band at the next visit.

Why “it stopped working” is usually something else

When owners tell us their flea treatment does not work, the story is usually one of three things. A dose was late or skipped. The dog moved up a weight band and kept getting the old box. Or the fleas are living in the carpet, the car or on another animal that is not treated. Flea eggs and larvae sit in the house for weeks, so a treated dog can keep picking up new fleas from an untreated home. Washing bedding hot, vacuuming often and treating every pet in the house closes most of those gaps.

None of that means a product is perfect. Some dogs do better on a chew, some on a topical, and some need a different active ingredient. That is exactly why the switch should go through your vet. They know your dog’s history, what is going around locally and what is safe with any other medication.

How to check your dog in two minutes

Use a fine flea comb along the back, the base of the tail and the belly. Wipe the comb on a damp white paper towel. Black specks that turn reddish-brown are flea dirt, which is digested blood. For ticks, run your fingers slowly over the head, ears, neck, armpits, between the toes and under the tail after any walk in grass or brush. Blue makes this easy because he will lie on the carpet for anyone who offers a belly rub. Most dogs warm up to it if you pair it with a treat.

If you find a tick, remove it with fine tweezers or a tick tool, grip close to the skin and pull straight out with steady pressure. Note the date. If your dog seems off in the following weeks, that date is useful for your vet.

Frequently asked questions

Does flea and tick prevention stop working over time?

Usually the cause is a missed dose, a dog that moved up a weight band, or fleas living in the home or on an untreated pet. If you still see fleas or ticks after fixing those, ask your vet about other options.
