Sleep on it: how to trial a new pillow before you commit

By the Pillow Exchange team · Last updated 23 September 2026 · 6 min read

You cannot judge a pillow by squeezing it in a shop or reading a product page. Firmness and height behave differently after six hours than after six seconds, and your neck has spent months or years adapting to your old pillow. That is why the first night on almost any new pillow feels strange — and why deciding on night one is how people end up rotating through a cupboard full of "wrong" pillows.

Why the first night lies

Several things are going on when you first sleep on a new pillow:

  • Your body is used to the old one. If your old pillow was too flat, a pillow of the right height can feel too tall at first, simply because it is different.
  • New fill is at its fullest. Many pillows, especially fibre and down, settle a little over the first week or two of use.
  • Foam changes with temperature. Memory foam feels firmer in a cold room and softens as it warms. A pillow unboxed on a chilly evening may feel harder than it will next week.
  • Compressed pillows need time to recover. Pillows shipped vacuum-packed or rolled often need a day or more to reach full shape. Check the instructions.
  • New smells and sounds. A fresh foam smell or the rustle of buckwheat can make you notice the pillow more than you will once it is familiar.

How long to give it

There is no official adjustment period, but many people find a new pillow starts to feel normal within a week or two. A reasonable approach is to commit to around two weeks unless the pillow is causing clear, worsening pain. If it is still uncomfortable after that, it is probably not the right one for you.

For adjustable pillows, use the first few nights to tune the fill. Start with more fill than you think you need and remove a handful at a time, leaving a night or two between changes so you can tell what made the difference. Keep the removed fill in a bag in case you want it back.

Keep a simple pillow diary

Memory is unreliable, especially about sleep. A few seconds of notes each morning turns "I think it is fine?" into a clear answer.

What you are looking for is the trend. Scores that start low and rise are a good sign of adjustment. Scores that stay low or fall, or a new pain appearing, suggest a mismatch. Note the direction of the problem too: "chin pushed down" means too high; "head tipping" means too low. That is exactly the information you need if you exchange it.

Signs it is a mismatch, not an adjustment

  • Pain that is sharper or more persistent than a mild stiffness that fades by mid-morning.
  • Numbness or tingling in the arm or hand that you did not have before.
  • You keep changing position to escape the pillow, rather than settling.
  • You are folding, stacking or pushing it aside by the second week.
  • Your diary scores are not improving after ten days or so.

If you get new pain, numbness or headaches that do not ease, stop using the pillow and, if they persist, speak to a healthcare professional.

Check the terms before you buy

Because pillows need real nights to judge, the trial and return terms matter as much as the pillow itself. Before you buy, look for:

  1. How long the trial or return window is, and whether it starts on the order date or delivery date.
  2. Whether used pillows can be returned or exchanged. Many retailers exclude bedding for hygiene reasons unless it is unopened.
  3. Who pays for return shipping, and whether a restocking fee applies.
  4. Whether you need the original packaging. Vacuum-packed pillows cannot be squeezed back in.
  5. Exchange versus refund: some offers only let you swap for a different pillow.

Frequently asked questions

Should I switch back to my old pillow some nights?

Try not to during the trial. Switching back and forth resets the adjustment and makes it harder to judge the new pillow fairly.

What if my partner and I disagree?

You do not need the same pillow. Different sleep positions and builds call for different pillows; trial yours on your own terms.

Is waking up sore on the first morning normal?

Mild stiffness that eases within an hour or so can happen as your neck adjusts to a different position. Sharp or lasting pain is not something to push through.

Good to know: this is general information about pillows and sleep habits, not medical advice. Ongoing pain, numbness, snoring with pauses in breathing, or sleep problems that last for weeks are worth raising with a doctor or physiotherapist.
