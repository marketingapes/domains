Darker bedroom before the clocks fall back

In most of the US, clocks fall back one hour on Sunday, November 1, 2026. Your alarm stays where it is, but sunrise suddenly lands an hour earlier on the clock. If daylight already creeps in before you mean to wake, a darker room is the simplest fix.

Three quick checks

  1. Find the light leaks. On a sunny morning, look for light around the edges and top of the window, not just through the fabric.
  2. Cover small lights. Chargers, TVs and smoke-alarm LEDs add up in a dark room. See our sleep habits guide.
  3. Decide: fix the window or fix your eyes. Curtains darken the room for everyone; an eye mask is cheap and travels with you.

Option 1: blackout curtains

“Blackout” fabric blocks light through the cloth, but light still sneaks around the sides. Panels that are wider than the window and hang a little above and below it help most.

Why an earlier sunrise can wake you

Light is the strongest signal your body clock uses to decide when the day starts. Even through closed eyelids, a brightening room can nudge you out of the lighter stages of sleep that cluster toward morning. After the time change, the same sunrise arrives an hour earlier by the clock, so a room that felt dark enough at 6:30 last week may start glowing well before your alarm. The fix is not complicated: reduce how much light reaches your eyes until you actually want to be up.

Close the gaps before you buy anything

Most bright bedrooms leak light around the window rather than through it. Before spending money, try a few no-cost or low-cost tweaks and see how far they get you:

  • Overlap the panels. If two curtains meet in the middle, a thin bright line often runs down the center. Overlapping them by a few inches, or clipping them together with a binder clip, closes it.
  • Raise and widen the rod. Mounting the rod several inches above the frame and extending it past each side lets the fabric cover the edges where light spills.
  • Check the top. A gap between the rod and the wall acts like a skylight. A wraparound rod or a simple valance blocks it.
  • Layer what you already own. Existing blinds behind a curtain, or a curtain behind a blind, cut more light than either alone.

How to measure for made-to-measure curtains

Custom panels only help if they fit. Measure the window width inside the frame, then add extra on each side so the fabric overlaps the wall; many retailers suggest adding several inches per side, and following the specific maker's sizing guide is the safest bet. For length, decide whether you want the panel to stop just below the sill or fall to the floor. Floor-length panels block more light from below and tend to look tidier. Write down the measurements for each window separately, because older homes rarely have identical frames.

Also think about how the curtain will open. Panels that stack beside the window when open keep the glass clear during the day, while panels that bunch over the frame steal some daylight. If you nap or work night shifts, prioritize full coverage over looks.

Small lights that add up

Once the window is handled, the remaining glow usually comes from inside the room. A strip of opaque tape over a charger LED, turning a clock face away from the bed, or moving a router out of the bedroom can make a surprising difference. Keep a dim, warm night light for safe trips to the bathroom instead of switching on a bright overhead lamp, which can make it harder to fall back asleep.

Easing into the new schedule

A dark room helps most when your routine also adjusts. In the days around the change, try to keep a steady wake time, get outdoor light soon after you get up, and dim screens and lamps in the last hour before bed. Kids and pets often feel the shift most, so moving their bedtime and meal times gradually, in small steps over several days, can make the first week smoother for the whole household.

Which option suits you

If you share the room, have pets that wake at first light, or simply dislike anything on your face, curtains are the better long-term fix. If you travel often, rent and cannot change hardware, or only need help for a few weeks, an eye mask is the low-commitment choice. Plenty of people use both: curtains for the room and a mask for the occasional early sunrise or afternoon nap.
